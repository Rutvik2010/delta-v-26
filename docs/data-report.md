# Space weather data audit

`engine/donki.js` requests solar-flare (FLR) records from [NASA CCMC DONKI](https://ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/tools/DONKI/) for the previous three UTC days. It first tries CCMC's API, then `api.nasa.gov` with `DEMO_KEY`. Each request has a 5-second timeout. It maps the strongest reported class to calm (A/B/C or none), active (M), or storm (X). The result reports `source: "live"` only after a successful API response. The engine does not call `getSpaceWeather` itself; the documented weather override applies at game start, and `nextSol` rolls weather independently after that.

If both live requests fail, time out, or return invalid data, `getSpaceWeather` randomly picks one entry from `data/space_weather_fallback.json` and reports `source: "embedded"`. If that file also fails, it returns embedded calm. A successful empty feed also maps to live calm. A malformed record with a missing or unfamiliar class maps to calm rather than being rejected. The fallback's 20 dated 2026 records are explicitly labeled made-up samples. Their C, M, and X classes are plausible flare categories, but their dates and strengths are not NASA measurements; random selection ignores the dates. It is unsuitable as historical evidence.

`data/flares_2024_05.json` is absent from this checkout, so its dates and classes cannot be audited. The actual May 2024 storm began with M- and X-class flares on May 8, and major activity continued through May 14 according to [NASA CCMC's period record](https://ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/TP/2024-05-TP-02/) and [NASA's storm summary](https://science.nasa.gov/science-research/heliophysics/how-nasa-tracked-the-most-intense-solar-storm-in-decades/). No claim about the missing local file's accuracy is possible.
